About Guimaras

Guimaras
Is a small island province in Western Visayas, Philippines, that proves small size does not mean small impact. Located in the Iloilo Strait between the islands of Panay and Negros, it is just a 15-minute ferry ride from Iloilo City, making it one of the most accessible island escapes in the country. Despite being the smallest province in the region in terms of land area, Guimaras has built a world-class reputation that reaches far beyond the Philippines.

Is famously known as the Mango Capital of the Philippines, and for good reason. The Guimaras mango is considered one of the sweetest in the world. It has a distinct aroma, smooth, fiber-free flesh, and a perfect balance of sweetness that no other mango can easily replicate. This is not by accident. The provincial government strictly prohibits the importation of mangoes from other provinces to protect the island from mango pests like the pulp weevil and seed weevil. Because of this strict quarantine, Guimaras mangoes are one of the few mango varieties that can be exported to countries with very strict agricultural standards, such as the United States and Australia. For Guimarasnons, the mango is not just a fruit, it is a source of pride and livelihood.

Is more than just mangoes. The island is blessed with stunning natural beauty. Its coastline is dotted with white sand beaches and crystal-clear waters. Alubihod Beach in Nueva Valencia is one of the most popular, perfect for swimming and sunset watching. From there, tourists can go island-hopping to nearby islets like Ave Maria Islet, with its small chapel and powdery sandbar, and Turtle Island. Inland, there are caves to explore, hills to hike, and marine sanctuaries ideal for snorkeling and diving.

The province is composed of five municipalities:
Jordan, which serves as the capital, Buenavista, Nueva Valencia, San Lorenzo, and Sibunag. Each town has its own character, but all share the island's peaceful, laid-back atmosphere. Life in Guimaras moves slower compared to the busy cities nearby, which is exactly why many travelers love it.

History:
Is also preserved here. In Buenavista, you can find the Navalas Church, the oldest surviving church in Guimaras, built in 1880. Its stone structure and antique beauty tell stories of the Spanish colonial era. Another important landmark is the Trappist Monastery in Jordan, where monks produce famous mango products like dried mangoes, mango jam, and mango ketchup, which have become favorite pasalubong items.

One of the most modern sights in Guimaras:
Is the Guimaras Wind Farm in San Lorenzo. With 27 towering wind turbines standing along the hills facing the sea, it is the largest wind farm in the Visayas. It not only provides clean and renewable energy for the island but also offers a breathtaking view that has become a top tourist attraction. The combination of green hills, blue sea, and giant white turbines is truly unforgettable.

The people pf Guimaras:
Called Guimarasnons, are known for their warmth, simplicity, and strong sense of community. Their hospitality makes visitors feel immediately welcome. Every May, the island comes alive with the Manggahan Festival, a colorful and joyful celebration of thanksgiving for the abundant mango harvest. The streets are filled with street dancing, floats decorated with mangoes, cultural shows, and of course, all-you-can-eat mangoes.

In a world of crowded tourist destinations, Guimaras offers something different: peace, sweetness, and authenticity. It is an island where you can relax on a quiet beach in the morning, taste the world's sweetest mango in the afternoon, and watch wind turbines spin against a golden sunset in the evening. Small, sweet, and serene, Guimaras is proof that the best things really do come in small packages.

Signature dishes

Mango Beef Bulalo:
Is a famous culinary innovation from Guimaras Island, popularized by homegrown establishments like The Pitstop Restaurant. It infuses the rich, savory broth of traditional Filipino beef shank soup with the natural sweetness and tang of Guimaras' world-renowned ripe mangoes, cutting through the oiliness of the marrow.
Ingredients:

For Beef:
1 kg beef shanks with bone marrow (bulalo cut)
1 kg beef brisket, cubed
3 liters water
1 large onion, quartered
1 tbsp whole peppercorns
1 tbsp rock salt / patis to taste

For Mango & Veggies:
1 large semi-ripe Guimaras mango - hiwain, medyo maasim pa (huwag hinog na hinog)
1 ripe Guimaras mango - para sa tamis, pang balance
2 corn on the cob, cut into 3 parts
1 bundle pechay / bok choy
2 medium patatas, quartered
1 small cabbage, quartered
2 tanglad, bugbugin (optional pero mas bango)
3 cloves garlic, crushed
1 thumb ginger, sliced

Toppings/Sawsawan:
Patis, kalamansi, sili

Step-by-Step Cooking Process

1. Blanch the Beef Shanks (Crucial for a Clear Broth)
Place the beef shanks in a large pot and fill it with enough cold water to fully cover the meat.
Bring it to a rolling boil over medium-high heat for about 10 minutes. You will see brown scum, blood, and impurities rise to the top.
Drain the water completely and rinse the beef shanks thoroughly under cold running water to clean off any remaining sediment. Wash the pot to ensure it's spotless.

2. Slow Simmer the Meat

Return the washed beef shanks back into the clean pot.
Pour in 8 to 10 cups of fresh cold water.
Add the quartered onion, crushed garlic, and whole black peppercorns.
Bring to a boil, then immediately reduce the heat to low. Cover the pot and let it simmer for 2 to 3 hours until the meat is fork-tender and melting off the bone. (Note: If using a pressure cooker, this will only take 40-45 minutes).
Periodically skim off any excess fat or light foam that floats to the surface to keep the soup clean.

3. Infuse the Mango and Seasonings

Pour in the fish sauce (patis) and add a pinch of salt to build the savory base.
Stir in the mango purée/mashed mango directly into the broth. The ripe mango dissolves smoothly into the hot soup, tinting the broth slightly yellow and infusing it with a unique, subtle fruity tang that cuts through the beef fat.

4. Cook the Hearty Vegetables

Add the sliced sweet corn chunks. Simmer for 10 to 12 minutes until the corn is tender.
Drop in the green beans and let them cook for 3 minutes.

5. Add the Final Greens and Mango Cubes

Turn off the heat or lower it to a bare minimum.
Add the cabbage or bok choy leaves, allowing the residual heat of the soup to wilt the greens while keeping them crisp (about 2 minutes).
Gently toss in the fresh ripe mango cubes right at the very end so they warm through but do not break apart or turn into mush.

6. Serve Hot

Ladle the rich, steaming broth into a wide serving bowl, making sure every portion gets a generous section of bone marrow, tender beef, vibrant vegetables, and sweet mango slices.
Serve immediately alongside hot steamed rice and a side dipping dish of fish sauce, squeezed calamansi, and crushed red chilis.
Kinilaw with Mango:
A popular local variation of the traditional Filipino raw seafood ceviche in Guimaras, where fresh catch like tanigue (Spanish mackerel) or tuna is "cooked" in vinegar and citrus, then tossed with crisp green or manibalang (semi-ripe) Guimaras mangoes.
Ingredients:

500g fresh fish fillet (preferably Tanigue / Spanish Mackerel or Tuna), cut into 1-inch cubes
1 large Guimaras green mango (or semi-ripe manibalang), peeled and cubed or thinly grated
½ cup Sukang Tuba (Native Coconut Vinegar) for the final marinade
½ cup extra vinegar (solely for washing/purifying the fish)
1 medium red onion, finely chopped or thinly sliced
1 thumb-sized ginger, julienned into fine strips
3-4 pieces Siling Labuyo (bird's eye chili), chopped
2 tbsp Calamansi juice (optional, for citrus brightness)
3 tbsp Fresh Coconut Cream (Gata), optional Visayan style to balance the sharpness
½ tsp salt and ¼ tsp ground black pepper to taste.

Step-by-Step Preparation Process

1. The Vinegar Wash (Cleaning)

Place your cubed fresh fish into a large glass or ceramic bowl. Pour over the ¾ cup of coconut vinegar. Gently toss the fish for exactly 2 minutes. You will see the surface of the fish slightly change color. Pour the fish into a fine-mesh strainer and drain the vinegar completely, pressing gently with the back of a spoon. Discard this vinegar. This initial wash removes any lingering fishy smell and cleans the meat.

2. Prep the Aromatics

In a separate, clean mixing bowl, toss together your sliced red onion, ginger, and chopped chilies. If you are using green mango, you can add it here as well.

3. The Citrus and Vinegar Cure

Add the washed fish cubes to the bowl of aromatics. Pour in your remaining ⅓ cup of clean coconut vinegar and the calamansi juice. Season generously with salt and black pepper. Mix well until every cube of fish is coated.

4. Toss in the Guimaras Mango

If using semi-ripe or sweet mangoes, fold the mango cubes gently into the mixture now. (Adding them at this stage prevents them from turning mushy while tossing). If you want an authentic Visayan twist, stir in the optional coconut cream at this stage to coat the fish in a velvety, milky sauce.

5. Cold Cure and Serve

Cover the bowl tightly with plastic wrap and place it in the refrigerator. Let it chill for 15 to 20 minutes. During this window, the acid will "cook" (denature) the fish proteins until the flesh turns beautiful opaque white. Avoid over-marinating past an hour, or the fish will lose its tender bounce and become chalky.
Savory Mango:
Savory mango dishes in Guimaras blend the island's world-famous sweet carabao mangoes with rich, cheesy, or spiced culinary creations.
Ingredients:

400g pork kasim (shoulder), cut into cubes
1 whole garlic, minced
1 red onion, sliced
1 ripe Guimaras mango, sliced
¼ cup soy sauce
½ cup vinegar
Water or broth
Cooking oil, salt, and pepper to taste

Step-by-Step Process

1. Sauté aromatics:

Heat a little oil in a pan over medium heat. Sauté the minced garlic and chopped red onion until fragrant and soft.

2. Brown the pork:

Add the cubed pork kasim to the pan. Season with ground black pepper and a splash of soy sauce, then sauté until the pork turns light brown and renders its natural fats.

3. Simmer with soy sauce:

Pour in the soy sauce and a small amount of water or stock. Cover the pan and let it simmer until the pork kasim becomes tender.

4. Add the vinegar:

Pour in the vinegar. Do not stir immediately; let it boil uncovered for a few minutes to cook through and balance the acidity.

5. Incorporate the mango:

Toss in the fresh Guimaras mango slices. Simmer gently for another 3 to 5 minutes until the mango softens slightly and blends its sweet juices into the savory adobo sauce.

6. Serve:

Remove from heat and serve hot over a cup of steamed white rice.
