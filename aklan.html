Top 5 Aklan Cuisine

Aklan cuisine reflects the rich culture and traditions of Aklan. From savory native chicken dishes to sweet rice delicacies, these foods showcase the unique flavors of the province.

🍗 Inubarang Manok

Inubarang Manok is a traditional Aklanon dish made with native chicken and ubad, or banana pith. It is cooked with coconut milk, lemongrass, garlic, ginger, and other spices.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole native chicken, cut into pieces
  • 2 cups ubad (banana pith)
  • 2 cups coconut milk
  • 2 stalks lemongrass
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 onion
  • 1 thumb-sized ginger
  • Salt and pepper
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil

How to Cook

  1. Heat oil in a large pot.
  2. Sauté garlic, onion, and ginger.
  3. Add the native chicken and cook until lightly browned.
  4. Add the ubad and lemongrass.
  5. Pour in the coconut milk.
  6. Season with salt and pepper.
  7. Cover and simmer until the chicken is tender.
  8. Serve hot with steamed rice.

🥥 Chicken Binakol / Binakoe

Chicken Binakol is a traditional dish made from native chicken, young coconut water, coconut meat, ginger, and lemongrass. It has a naturally sweet and refreshing flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole native chicken
  • 2 cups young coconut water
  • 1 cup young coconut meat
  • 1 thumb-sized ginger
  • 2 stalks lemongrass
  • 1 onion
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • Salt and pepper
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil

How to Cook

  1. Heat oil and sauté garlic, onion, and ginger.
  2. Add the chicken and cook until slightly brown.
  3. Pour in the young coconut water.
  4. Add the lemongrass and young coconut meat.
  5. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Cover and simmer until the chicken becomes tender.
  7. Adjust the seasoning.
  8. Serve hot with rice.

🦐 Linapay / Tinamuk

Linapay, also known as Tinamuk, is a traditional Aklanon dish made from pounded freshwater shrimp and young coconut meat. The mixture is wrapped in taro leaves and cooked in coconut milk.

Ingredients

  • 500g freshwater shrimp
  • 1 cup young coconut meat
  • 10 taro leaves
  • 2 cups coconut milk
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 onion
  • 1 thumb-sized ginger
  • Salt and pepper

How to Cook

  1. Clean and pound the freshwater shrimp.
  2. Mix the shrimp with young coconut meat.
  3. Add garlic, onion, ginger, salt, and pepper.
  4. Place a portion of the mixture on a taro leaf.
  5. Wrap the mixture securely with the leaf.
  6. Arrange the wrapped mixture in a cooking pot.
  7. Pour coconut milk over the wrapped portions.
  8. Simmer until fully cooked.
  9. Serve warm.

🍚 Inday-Inday

Inday-Inday is a sweet Aklanon delicacy made from sticky rice flour. It is commonly served with sweetened coconut or bukayo.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups glutinous rice flour
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • ½ cup water
  • Banana leaves

How to Cook

  1. Mix glutinous rice flour with water to form a soft dough.
  2. Shape the dough into small pieces.
  3. Boil water in a pot.
  4. Cook the rice dough pieces until they float.
  5. Remove and drain them.
  6. Cook brown sugar with water to make a sweet syrup.
  7. Add grated coconut to the syrup.
  8. Serve the Inday-Inday with the sweet coconut topping.

🍰 Latik

Latik is a traditional Aklan rice delicacy made with glutinous rice, coconut, and sugar. It is a popular sweet treat and can be wrapped in banana leaves.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups glutinous rice
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • ½ cup water
  • Banana leaves

How to Cook

  1. Wash the glutinous rice.
  2. Cook the rice with coconut milk.
  3. Stir until the rice becomes soft and sticky.
  4. In another pan, combine brown sugar and water.
  5. Cook until the sugar becomes a thick syrup.
  6. Add grated coconut and mix well.
  7. Combine the sweet coconut mixture with the sticky rice.
  8. Wrap portions in banana leaves.
  9. Let them cool before serving.
