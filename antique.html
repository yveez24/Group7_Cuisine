About Antique

Stretching along the western coast of Panay Island, Antique is a coastal province in the Western Visayas bounded by mountain ranges to the east and the open expanse of the Sulu Sea to the west. Established in 1780, it shares borders with Aklan, Capiz, and Iloilo, with the bustling town of San Jose de Buenavista serving as its provincial capital and economic heart. Antique’s unique geography—a long, narrow strip of land where lush highlands drop directly into pristine coastal waters—has sheltered its distinct identity for centuries, giving rise to a close-knit community deeply connected to both land and sea.

Grounded in Kinaray-a traditions, folk legends, and historic muscovado sugar crafting, Antique boasts a rich cultural heritage. Its unpretentious culinary scene centers on time-honored cooking methods—like slow-simmering in fresh coconut milk, steaming in banana leaves, and cooking in bamboo—celebrating fresh local harvests and ancestral recipes passed down through generations.

Local Food Guide

Signature Dishes & Delicacies

Local dishes and delicacies from Antique.

Porok or Porbida

Porbida is a classic regional dish featuring chicken simmered in coconut milk, tomatoes, aromatics, annatto, and unique alupidan leaves. Savory, mildly creamy, and warmly spiced, it balances clean, recognizable flavors with a subtle earthy twist and an inviting reddish-golden hue.

Ginat-ang Tambo na may Patuyaw

“Ginat-an nga tambo kag patuyaw” or bamboo shoots and river shrimp in coconut milk is not just any dish. Local superstition says locals should carefully watch the cooking process to make sure that nothing spills over from the open pot or the cook dies in what is called “Angongan,” wherein an evil spirit chokes him while he sleeps

Bandi

Bandi (or Bandi Mani) is Antique’s signature peanut brittle, crafted from native roasted peanuts fused together with sweet, dark local muscovado sugar and finished with a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds. Flat, crunchy, and richly caramelized, this iconic sweet treat highlights the province’s prized muscovado heritage in every bite.

Banag nga may gata

Banag nga may Gata features native spiny freshwater snails simmered in rich coconut cream infused with local aromatic herbs. Distinctive for its long-spined shells, this traditional Antique dish pairs the tender, earthy flavor of the snails with a velvety, savory sauce.

Nilaga nga Tatus

Nilaga nga Tatus highlights the sweet, tender meat of Caluya, Antique’s prized coconut crab (*Tatus*)—the world’s largest land-living arthropod. Lightly boiled with salt and local aromatics like lemongrass, this simple preparation honors the crab's natural sweetness while celebrating the island's rich coastal heritage and culinary bounty.

Butong-Butong

Butong-Butong is Antique’s classic pulled muscovado candy, named after the local Hiligaynon/Kinaray-a word *butong*, meaning "to pull." Crafted by vigorously stretching warm, melted muscovado sugar until it turns into a soft, golden-white gooey sweet, this traditional treat showcases the island's rich sugar heritage with every chewy bite.

Binakol nga Manok

Binakol nga Manok is a signature specialty from Callan, Sebaste, featuring tender chicken simmered in fresh coconut juice, tender coconut meat, papaya, and chili leaves. Traditionally cooked inside fresh bamboo (kawayan) to infuse the broth with a distinct natural richness, this comforting soup offers a unique, sweet-savory taste of Antique’s vibrant culinary history.

Huwad-Huwad

Huwad-Huwad is an ultra-rare native dessert exclusive to the Antique towns of Belison and Tobias Fornier. Crafted from ground glutinous rice, rich coconut milk, and unrefined muscovado sugar steamed inside slender, banana-leaf tubes, this elusive sweet offers a delightfully authentic taste of Antique's hidden culinary treasures.

Binabak

Binabak is a prized Northern Antique delicacy made from pounded river shrimp (patuyaw) mixed with tender young coconut meat and fresh ginger, wrapped in banana leaves, and gently steamed. Famous for its delicate balance of sweet seafood, aromatic ginger, and rich coconut flavor, this iconic specialty was featured on *Biyahe ni Drew* as a true taste of local heritage.
